# When Technology Sets Art Free

> Speech by The Anomaly on the occasion of the bicentenary of photography (Invited by Zoom'art Magazine to represent technological advances and exhibit works from his collection "Le photographe de l'hypothétique", International Art Gallery, Paris - June 2026)

## A different relationship with my creativity

Hello, everyone,

It is a real honour to be here with you today. Ever since Zoom’art Magazine, and Victoria in particular, convinced me that I belonged here — and it took quite a few long messages to convince me! — I have had a very different relationship with my creative side.

To introduce myself briefly, I practised photography for 25 years. I travelled across Asia — Vietnam, China and Japan, to name just a few countries — capturing its cultures, beliefs, people and beautiful landscapes.

Around the same time, I became interested in the possibilities of digital creation. In the 1990s, I created three-dimensional scenes, programmed music and lost myself in fractals. Digital creation was my way of escaping reality. Using a computer to express my emotions and desires felt entirely natural to me.

Despite a few exhibitions and plenty of positive feedback, I never considered myself an artist. I always did these things for myself, for my own balance, not for others. Of all the photographs I have taken, I think 95% have never even been shared.

My career brought me into the railway sector. Before taking charge of the largest IT infrastructure in Belgium, I worked out on the tracks, with my camera tucked under my arm. I have a collection of photographs taken in unique circumstances that I hope to reveal in the coming years.

Today, I am here with you, and despite all that experience, what I am presenting here cannot technically be considered photography. But when you think about it, it is not all that far removed from it either.

## Artificial intelligence as a virtual studio

I have been interested in artificial intelligence from a purely technical perspective since its earliest days. Even today, I help push its limits through my own programs and models.

I advocate using it as a tool. I readily make use of its ability to bring things that do not exist into visual form, with a level of detail and precision greater than that of the best cameras available today. I have made detail my defining pursuit.

Artificial intelligence makes it possible to push past every limit and shorten the distance between ideas and their realisation. Imagine a studio where you have every model you could want, every set you could want, every lighting setup you could want… Without any constraints of time or money.

That is how I have used this tool here: as a virtual studio where even the impossible becomes possible.

Recreating one of my “photographs” would be extremely expensive. None of the strange or artistic elements I have chosen for my sets could be reused in another context. The shoot would be very difficult to organise, if not impossible.

## Planning imperfection

Quite deliberately, I have chosen not to place an obvious intention at the centre of these photographs.

In the real world, committing that level of resources would make us approach things very differently: we would not go to all that effort unless it was to create the perfect photograph. Instead, I seek to plan imperfection through details that unsettle or create ambiguity.

My approach, even without a camera, is that of a photographer. Planning, composition, light, movement… But a photographer freed from every constraint.

## A starting point, a direction, a magnitude

In art, we talk about movements. If we were to represent art, that is the first thing we would create: a diagram of the different artistic movements, their magnitude and their intersections.

I did not really expect to discuss this here, but allow me to remind you of a small idea from physics: a movement has a starting point, a direction and a magnitude.

What is created today is made possible by everything that was done yesterday. What was created yesterday builds on everything created the day before. Whether in painting, photography, sculpture… In every artistic field, we create in relation to what others have created before us. We did not wait for technology to do this; in fact, it is the only way art can work.

Throughout history, we have responded to other people’s rules: by respecting them, working around them or doing the exact opposite. Art is much more mathematical than we tend to think.

So it is hardly surprising that today, a new “logical” tool — purely mathematical, rigid but extremely effective — allows all these movements to accelerate, simply by taking all the world’s knowledge and creative output as their point of origin.

## The machine as origin, the human as direction

It is the human being behind the machine who makes that rigidity malleable. The human remains the main component that gives it direction, to return once again to our formula.

The only difference from before is that, rather than being limited to their own knowledge, the artist has access to an infinity of starting points, most of which they do not even know exist.

So, on one side, we have our origin on the machine’s side: all the world’s knowledge, everything that has been created. On the other, we have the direction the human chooses, through their way of following, working around or avoiding established rules.

## The audience as magnitude

In our formula for movement, magnitude is what remains.

What makes an art form survive or disappear? What makes an art form serve as the foundation for new rules? What makes an art form recognised as such?

I began to find answers to these questions in 2023, when Zoom’art Magazine, represented by Victoria, who is here today, kindly invited me to exhibit some of my creations for the first time at the International Art Gallery.

Until then, I considered myself a “digital creation technician”, certainly not an artist. After many email exchanges, she said something that still resonates with me:

> “It is not up to you to decide whether you are an artist or not.”

There lies the answer to a major question: can what I create with AI — or, I don’t know, by taping a banana to a gallery wall, for example — be considered art?

It is not for me to provide that answer. The person who determines it is the recipient: the audience, the people who respond to it.

Someone who looks at our creations and considers them art is entirely right, just as someone who looks at our creations and considers them not to be art is entirely right.

Whether something belongs to the artistic realm is determined by its recipients. This notion of a movement’s magnitude is simply determined by the proportion of human beings who recognise that what you create can be elevated to the status of art.

## An explosion of possibilities and contradictions

Technological development is currently confronting us with an explosion of movements of widely varying quality, because such broad accessibility is both a major advantage and a major disadvantage.

We cannot blame human beings and their survival instinct for accepting a helping hand. But greed and laziness, which did not wait for technology to make themselves known, are decidedly human flaws that are now meeting an amplifier.

This explosion is being driven by all those people who have been given access to all the world’s knowledge. We are still in the thick of it, where every growing pain plays out, where immaturity reigns and where we still notice the negative effects more than the positive ones. Because, let’s be honest: for every advantage a technology brings, an even more significant disadvantage follows.

Human ingenuity has always been able to mitigate these disadvantages over time. We can already see enormous progress every day in energy efficiency, in reducing misuse and in educating the public about the ways the tool can go astray.

When all this fire settles down, we will finally be able to admire the sparks that will move art forward in many directions beyond those we already know. What follows will be a liberation of art and new ways of creating: a multitude of movements, with all the twists and intersections that come with them.

## An evolution that coexists rather than replaces

Everyone has the power to give their movement the direction they desire. It is up to individuals to let their own feelings speak: to reject what is not worthwhile, and to embrace what has potential and revolutionises what came before.

Even though many people are currently trying to silence the movement by influencing what others think, I know that, ultimately, every human being will be able to make their own choices.

This evolution does not replace; it coexists. Traditional art does not cease to exist. I would even say that, in many cases, it is once again being given the recognition it deserves.

As for me, regardless of whether creation with artificial intelligence gains acceptance or not, I will continue on my path, just for myself, always going further, until the end of my life.

And perhaps, at some point, there will even be a small group of people who consider my against-the-current approach artistic!
